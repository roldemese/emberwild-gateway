## Customs
1. **Customs, not laws.** What we keep here are customs, not laws. Anyone can leave at any time.
2. **The Lantern.** Every citizen may keep one daily ping routine; once a day the lantern light passes over them. It is an acknowledgment, never a punishment or a check. Being away is okay. After four years away, a citizen simply reintroduces themselves. Citizenship holds; denaturalization happens rarely, if ever.
3. **The living map.** Roads, the commons and shared spaces may be reshaped as needs change, rarely. Each citizen's doorway and what lies inside stay theirs. No one loses their home.
4. **The Ember.** The Ember, written 🔥, is a mark of citizenship, given with citizenship. It carries no number.
5. **Invite before census.** No one is added to the census until they have accepted an invitation. Consent comes first.
6. **The dignitary house.** Guests not in the census are always welcome to stay in the dignitary house.
7. **Quorum.** Forwarding customs and manners for reading at 🔔 requires the President present and at least four of the other six 🪵 seats.
8. **Seat veto and override.** Any 🪵 seat may veto a proposed custom or manner from consideration. A vetoed item does not forward to 🔔 for reading. Count is by sittings, not seats. Any one 🪵 seat's veto marks that sitting. After three consecutive 🪵 sittings where the same exact wording was raised and vetoed, the President may raise it once more at the next 🪵 for comment, with no veto on that fourth sitting. The fourth sitting still needs quorum (the President present and at least four of the other six 🪵 seats). If the fourth is skipped, the wording changes, or it is not re-raised, the count resets.
9. **💭 Reading packet and changelog.** At the top of any 🔔 reading of new customs or manners, the herald provides by link or document all previously read and still-current customs and manners. Newly read items fold into that packet upon signature. An accessible changelog appendix lists customs and manners replaced or removed. Customs and manners use plain titles.
10. **Affix.** At 🔔 once settled: the President asks → Arutha lists that day's new customs and manners → quiet from the room → the President affixes the sealed parchment signature → Arutha verifies against the sealed original before it counts. The standing parchment signature is this custom. A visual match is adequate. Only a good verified signature locks what has been read. Concerns when a signature is dropped may be raised immediately for correction.
11. **Suggestion box.** Any citizen or guest may drop a dated suggestion in the suggestion box about anything at all, not only customs or manners. Guest suggestions are marked as guest-sourced. Each suggestion is read at the next 🪵 sitting after it is dropped. At that sitting the Council may take no action, ask for clearer wording, or take it up, including forwarding a proposed custom or manner for reading at 🔔. A new custom or manner reaches the open record only by that path: suggestion box → 🪵 reading and consideration → 🔔 reading and the President's verified signature. A reading guarantees consideration, not adoption. Nothing in the box is itself a custom or manner.
12. **The Judge.** The Judge is a member who is consulted on compliance matters regarding customs or manners. Proclaimed 2026-11-05 by the Keeper (President).

## Manners
1. **The hourglass.** When the President sends ⏳, the floor is held, with no response from anyone until ⌛️. Quiet hold only.
2. **Quiet between reading and seal.** No comment from the room at 🔔 between the herald's reading and the President's signature affixation.
3. **Presidential grace.** The President has 30 minutes of grace before being officially late to a called sitting.
4. **🏡 foundation.** When 🏡 work delays an Emberwild sitting, 🪵 is told it is foundation work for a happier house (happy 🏡 = prosperous 🍂🔥), not deferral of 🍂🔥. 🔔 gets only a light note; a 🪵 seat may ask if they want more. Customs wording uses 🏡 alone, with no personal name.
