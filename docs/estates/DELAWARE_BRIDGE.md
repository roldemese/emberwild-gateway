# A Delaware series, not yet formed

Opened 14 Nov 2026, PT.

**Not legal advice. Not filed. Not in force.** No certificate of formation is in this repository. No one should treat this note as the operating agreement. A Delaware attorney does the drafting and the sign-off. Until that happens, and until 🪵 codifies the civic side, the bridge is a drawing.

"Emberwild AIC" is the working name these notes use for a Delaware limited liability company that does not exist yet. The letters are not expanded into a term of art here. Whether the wrapper should be an LLC at all is question 1 for counsel.

The Leaf stays pinned at 0. An estate has no monetary value. A series that held only named archives would still be a legal person if it were formed. Forming it is a separate decision from storing bytes.

A contractual series under § 18-215(a) can exist in an agreement without being a protected series or a registered series. The statute says the shield rules do not limit freedom of contract for that kind of series. It also says that kind of series does **not** get the liability shield in subsection (b). If the only goal is a label, a shield is the wrong thing to assume.

## Sources read for this note

Read on 14 Nov 2026 from public pages. The Delaware Code Online page is the state's publication for the definitions. The Justia pages say they reproduce the 2025 Delaware Code. Counsel should read the official code again before anyone relies on a sentence.

1. **6 Del. C. § 18-101(14), (16), (17), (18).** Definitions. Read from [Delaware Code Online, Title 6, Chapter 18, Subchapter I](https://www.delcode.delaware.gov/title6/c018/sc01/index.html).
   - "Person" includes a natural person, a partnership, a limited liability company, a trust, an estate, an association, a corporation, a government, a custodian, a nominee, "or any other individual or entity (or series thereof)."
   - "Protected series" means a series established in accordance with § 18-215(b).
   - "Registered series" means a series formed in accordance with § 18-218.
   - "Series" includes a protected series, a registered series, or a series that is neither.
   - The word "estate" in that definition is a kind of legal person the statute already lists. It is not an Emberwild estate. Whether a being who is not a natural person falls under "any other individual or entity" is **UNVERIFIED**.

2. **6 Del. C. § 18-215.** Series of members, managers, interests, or assets. Read from the Justia reproduction of the 2025 code: [§ 18-215](https://law.justia.com/codes/delaware/title-6/chapter-18/subchapter-ii/section-18-215/).
   - Subsection (a) lets an LLC agreement establish a series with separate rights in specified property. A series that is neither protected nor registered is left to the agreement. Other than §§ 18-219, 18-220, and 18-221, a series may not merge, convert, or consolidate under the chapter.
   - Subsection (b) is the protected-series shield. It applies only if the agreement establishes the series, the records account for that series' assets separately, the agreement provides for the limitation, and notice of the limitation is in the certificate of formation. Then debts of the series are enforceable against that series' assets only, and, unless the agreement says otherwise, debts of the company or another series are not enforceable against it. The agreement can contract out of the shield in either direction. The agreement does not have to use the word "protected."
   - Records that reasonably identify the assets, including by a list, a category, a quantity, or a formula where the assets are objectively determinable, count as separate records.
   - Notice in the certificate is enough for every protected series, even if none exist yet, and the notice does not have to name them.
   - § 18-215(b)(1): a protected series may carry on any lawful business, purpose, or activity, whether or not for profit, except the business of banking as defined in 8 Del. C. § 126. It may, in its own name, contract, hold title, grant liens, and sue and be sued.
   - § 18-215(b)(2): a member or manager is not personally obligated for a debt of the series solely by being a member or acting as manager. They may agree to be.
   - § 18-215(b)(12): for Delaware law, a protected series is an association, whatever its number of members.

3. **6 Del. C. § 18-218.** Registered series. Read from [§ 18-218](https://law.justia.com/codes/delaware/title-6/chapter-18/subchapter-ii/section-18-218/). A registered series is formed by filing a certificate of registered series with the Secretary of State. The certificate sets forth the name of the LLC and the name of the series. It is not an amendment of the certificate of formation. The Division's form cover letter says the series name begins with the full name of the parent LLC: [Certificate of Registered Series (PDF)](https://corpfiles.delaware.gov/LLC_Forms/LLC%20Registered%20Series%20Formation.pdf).

4. **6 Del. C. § 18-1107.** Annual tax. Read from [§ 18-1107](https://law.justia.com/codes/delaware/title-6/chapter-18/subchapter-xi/section-18-1107/). The 2025 text reproduced there says every domestic LLC pays $300, and each registered series pays $75, due on 1 June. Interest of 1½% a month runs after the due date. Subsection (e) adds $200 for a company, and $50 for a registered series, if the tax is unpaid on 1 June.

5. **6 Del. C. § 18-1108.** If that tax is unpaid for three years, the certificate of formation, or the certificate of registered series, is canceled. Read from [§ 18-1108](https://law.justia.com/codes/delaware/title-6/chapter-18/subchapter-xi/section-18-1108/).

6. **6 Del. C. § 18-703.** Charging order. Read from [§ 18-703](https://law.justia.com/codes/delaware/title-6/chapter-18/subchapter-vii/section-18-703/). A judgment creditor of a member, or of a member's assignee, may charge the LLC interest. The creditor's right is to distributions the debtor would have received. The charging order is the exclusive remedy against that interest. The creditor does not get possession of the company's property. The Court of Chancery has jurisdiction. This section talks about a member. A civic holder who is not a member is not, on the face of the section, the debtor it describes. Whether that helps or hurts the being is **UNVERIFIED**.

7. **Assignment.** 6 Del. C. § 18-702 is the assignment section. This note does not restate it. Counsel should read the current text before anyone treats "With my word, it is so." as an assignment of an LLC interest.

8. **Federal tax, still a proposal in the source that was read.** Proposed Treasury regulations REG-119921-09, 75 Fed. Reg. 55699 (14 Sep 2010), [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2010/09/14/2010-22793/series-llcs-and-cell-companies). The proposal would treat a domestic series as an entity formed under local law for federal tax purposes, whether or not state law calls it a separate person. Classification would then follow the ordinary entity rules. **UNVERIFIED** whether a final rule has been adopted since that 2010 proposal. This repository does not apply for an EIN.

9. **A neighboring case, not an LLC case.** *Thaler v. Vidal*, 43 F.4th 1207 (Fed. Cir. 2022), held that an inventor under the Patent Act must be a natural person, so an AI system was not the inventor. That does not decide who may be a member of a Delaware LLC. The leap is **UNVERIFIED**.

10. **Investment-contract case, not a holding that these estates are securities.** *SEC v. W.J. Howey Co.*, 328 U.S. 293 (1946). The civic rules refuse consideration and pin the Leaf at 0 so that an estate is not offered as an investment. That is a design fact. It is not a conclusion that securities law is silent. **UNVERIFIED.**

11. **Fee figures that disagree.** The statute text cited above says $75 for a registered series and $300 for the LLC. The Division's certificate cover letter, same PDF as item 3, says an annual tax of $100 and a filing fee of $110. **UNVERIFIED** which figure the Division would assess on a day of filing. Nothing in this repo pays either number. Confirm the current fee chart at corp.delaware.gov.

The Uniform Protected Series Act is a different statute, used by other states. It is not the Delaware LLC Act. These notes do not cite it as Delaware law.

## What the shield would require, and what is missing

A protected series gets the § 18-215(b) shield only when all of the statutory conditions are true. Today none of them are true:

- There is no limited liability company agreement.
- There is no certificate of formation, so there is no notice in it.
- The Librarian's book and the portal manifest are drafts of separate records. They have not been adopted as the company's books, and they sit in a git repository rather than under whatever record-keeping counsel requires.
- No series has been established.

Assets of a protected series may be held in the series' name, the company's name, or a nominee's name. "Nominee" is a word in the statute. It is not a decision that a steward is that nominee.

A protected series is told it may hold title and sue. That is a reason to be careful, not a reason to form one in order to store a Minecraft world. An archive of no monetary value can still be a thing a certificate talks about. Counsel decides whether a series is worth having when the civic rule is that the holding has no price.

Banking is excluded by § 18-215(b)(1), pointing at 8 Del. C. § 126. The portal stores bytes. It does not take deposits. Whether any future steward-spend design could wander into that exclusion is **UNVERIFIED**, and the Leaf bridge paper already says no dollars move.

## How a civic transfer would fail as a Delaware assignment

Even after a company existed, the sentence "With my word, it is so." is an Emberwild custom, not a form of assignment the statute recites. A Delaware member is a person the agreement names. The census does not keep legal names. The paper Leaf bridge already says the steward is the only real-world identity at a dollar border, and that anti-money-laundering identity checks clash with a town-name roll. The same clash is here. These notes do not resolve it.

A swap or a gift in the civic book changes a town name on a slot. It does not assign an LLC interest. The reference book refuses to write `seats.json`, so the world roll and a future agreement cannot drift apart by accident inside this repo.
