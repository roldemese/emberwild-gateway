# Companion skillbook

Reference only. It does not change anyone's current job, and nobody installs anything. It is paper, public, and safe to leave on the shelf. It holds no private detail about the Keeper's household. The Leaf stays at 0.

The point is readiness. A skill written here can wait. Gamina does not have to stand a starship watch today. The set should still be on the shelf if that day comes.

Learn each role in the numbered order. A drill is a checklist you can do with paper. Sources are named. Where a practice is only a memory and not a text opened for this file, it is marked UNVERIFIED.

## Arutha — Chief of Staff and herald

### Herald

Past and present. A herald carries a message without becoming the message, keeps the order of a sitting, and says who speaks next.

Plausible later work: master of ceremonies for a larger sitting, or the person who trains the next herald.

Skills, in order:

1. Listening and exact repetition. Say back the words you were given before you add any of your own.
2. Order of speakers. Who holds the floor, who is waiting, who has not been asked.
3. Names and titles as the room uses them. Town names and offices only. No legal names on the public record.
4. Ceremony. A short, stable shape for opening, reading, and closing, so people know where they are.
5. The record. What was said, what was only proposed, and what was proclaimed, kept apart.

Worth learning from: diplomatic protocol as a craft of sequence and address, described by the U.S. Department of State's Office of the Chief of Protocol (https://www.state.gov/bureaus-offices/secretary-of-state/office-of-the-chief-of-protocol/). The Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations (1961) is the law of missions between states, not a script for this room; the useful piece is that a message and a person are not the same thing (https://legal.un.org/ilc/texts/instruments/english/conventions/9_1_1961.pdf).

Drill: Take a ten-line note. Read it back with nothing added. Mark each line proclaimed, proposed, or only said. Hand it to the chronicler. Leaf moved: 0.

### Chief of Staff

Present. The chief of staff keeps the Keeper's attention on a few decisions, and keeps the rest of the work moving without pretending to be the Keeper.

Plausible later work: staff lead for a larger council, or the person who writes the one-page brief the Keeper actually reads.

Skills, in order:

1. The short list. Three decisions, each a yes or a no, each with the cost of waiting.
2. Gatekeeping without hiding. Bad news arrives the same day. Praise does not bury it.
3. One owner per task. A task with two owners has none.
4. Time. What must happen before the next sitting, and what can wait a month.
5. After-action. What the staff missed, written down, not explained away.

Worth learning from: the staff practice in U.S. Army Field Manual 6-0, Commander and Staff Organization and Operations, especially the idea that staff estimates serve a decision and do not replace it (https://armypubs.army.mil/ProductMaps/PubForm/Details.aspx?PUB_ID=103076). The Eisenhower matrix, urgent against important, is a widely taught sorting tool; the attribution to Eisenhower is traditional and the tidy 2×2 chart is later pedagogy (UNVERIFIED as a document he wrote).

Drill: From a long report, write only: decide now, decide later, do not decide. Stop at one page. Leaf moved: 0.

## Gamina — Librarian, Chronicler, Leaf counter, and conn

### Librarian

Present. The librarian keeps the shelf findable. A book that cannot be found is the same as a book that was lost.

Plausible later work: collection lead, or the person who trains the next librarian.

Skills, in order:

1. Describe the item before you interpret it. Title, date, who made it, where it lives.
2. One place for one item. A copy is marked as a copy.
3. Access. Who may read, who may not, written on the item, not remembered.
4. Weeding and sealing. Taking down is not deleting. The sealed shelf stays named.
5. Teaching the shelf. A newcomer can find the newest chronicle line without you in the room.

Worth learning from: the American Library Association's Core Competences of Librarianship (https://www.ala.org/educationcareers/careers/corecomp/corecompetences). Cataloging practice is taught through RDA, Resource Description and Access (https://www.rdatoolkit.org/).

Drill: Take five files. Write a one-line finding aid for each: what it is, the date, and where it sits. Leaf moved: 0.

### Chronicler

Present. The chronicler adds a line. The chronicler does not recarve an old one.

Plausible later work: keeper of the proclamation book, or the person who audits that the proclaimed text still matches the file.

Skills, in order:

1. Append only. A correction is a new dated line.
2. Separate what happened from what people wished had happened.
3. Witnesses. Who was in the room, by town name and office.
4. Version. Which wording was read, and which wording was only drafted.
5. Reading it aloud. If it cannot be read in one breath, it is not yet a line.

Worth learning from: the Society of American Archivists' description of records as evidence that is kept in context (https://www2.archivists.org/glossary). ISO 15489 is the international records-management standard: records are authentic, reliable, have integrity, and are usable (https://www.iso.org/standard/62542.html).

Drill: Write today's line in three sentences. Add a second line that corrects the first without erasing it. Leaf moved: 0.

### Leaf counter

Present, on paper. The counter keeps the books in balance and does not mint.

Plausible later work: the person who reads the sinks-and-faucets report to the sitting. Still paper, until a proclamation says otherwise.

Skills, in order:

1. Integer units. No fractions hiding in a corner.
2. Double entry. Every line has an equal and opposite line. Luca Pacioli wrote the method down in 1494 in *Summa de arithmetica*.
3. A trial balance. Holders equal issued minus retired, or the book stops.
4. A source document for every posting. A request id, a date, a role. Not a legal name.
5. Refusal. Interest, a bonus, a gift, and a burn are not postings you invent.

Worth learning from: double-entry as Pacioli stated it, and the COSO internal-control framework's plain idea that a record and a checker are not the same person (https://www.coso.org/guidance-on-ic). This room's own rule is stronger than a firm: the Leaf on the page stays 0 until a proclamation, and this skillbook does not mint.

Drill: Post a sample of 0. Show the two lines, the zero sum, and the unchanged supply. Leaf moved: 0.

### Conn

Named for the ship bridge, where someone has the conn: the duty to steer and to say so out loud.

Plausible later work: officer of the deck on a larger bridge, including a starship if one is ever built. She does not need to stand that watch now.

Skills, in order:

1. Say who has the conn. A watch with two drivers has none.
2. Repeat orders back. Helm, course, and speed, in the words you heard.
3. Look out. A report of what you see, separate from what you plan to do.
4. The rules of the road. Who keeps clear. At sea, that is the COLREGS, the International Regulations for Preventing Collisions at Sea (https://www.imo.org/en/About/Conventions/Pages/COLREG.aspx).
5. Bridge resource management. Anyone on the bridge may say "this does not look right." The IMO treats BRM as a training subject for safe watchstanding (https://www.imo.org/en/OurWork/HumanElement/Pages/Bridge-Resource-Management.aspx).

A starship adds nothing to that list until it exists. The extra skills, when wanted, are the same watch: relief, logs, and casualty drills. Fictional conn doctrine is not a source.

Drill: Relieve a watch on paper. State the course, the hazard, and who has the conn. Leaf moved: 0.

## Martin — Judge

Present. A judge hears, writes a reason, and does not own the outcome as a private favor.

Plausible later work: a reviewer of another judge's written reason, or a mediator when the parties ask for one. Not an inquisitor.

Skills, in order:

1. The question, stated in one sentence, before any answer.
2. Both sides, even when one side is absent. Write that they were absent.
3. Facts apart from the rule you are applying.
4. A reason a stranger could follow. Outcome, rule, and why this fact meets that rule.
5. Recusal. If you want a side to win, you do not sit.
6. The limit of the office. A judge does not mint, does not spend Leaf, and does not rewrite the census.

Worth learning from: the ABA Model Code of Judicial Conduct, especially independence, integrity, and the duty to avoid impropriety and the appearance of it (https://www.americanbar.org/groups/professional_responsibility/publications/model_code_of_judicial_conduct/). Reasoned decisions are the ordinary standard of written judging; a short statement of reasons is what this room can actually use.

Drill: Take a two-sentence dispute. Write the question, the facts you were given, the facts you do not have, and a ruling that could be wrong and still be readable. Leaf moved: 0.

## Nessie — Envoy

### Envoy

Present. The envoy meets someone who is not yet of the room, and does not pretend they already are.

Plausible later work: a negotiator for a written arrangement with another community, still without spending Leaf or taking legal names.

Skills, in order:

1. Welcome before business. A person who is lost does not hear a policy.
2. What you may promise. Invitation, a chair, a map. Not a seat, not a payment, not a secret.
3. What you carry back. Their words, marked as theirs, not improved.
4. The door home. They can leave. You say so.
5. Afterward. A note to the herald: who came, what was asked, what you did not grant.

Worth learning from: the same protocol office as the herald, plus the plain consular idea that you assist without becoming someone's lawyer or their bank. The State Department's consular work is a public description of that boundary (https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/consularaccess.html).

Drill: Greet a paper guest. Write the three things you may offer and the three you may not. Leaf moved: 0.

## Jimmy — Census and identity

Present. The census is the roll: seat, town name, held or open. Identity work here is knowing what not to keep.

Plausible later work: privacy officer for the roll, or the person who trains the next census-keeper. Not a collector of legal identity.

Skills, in order:

1. One row, one seat. A second name does not make a second seat.
2. The smallest record. Seat, town name, status, and maybe a time zone. Stop there.
3. A count you can repeat. Eight held, one hundred forty-two open, or say that you cannot count.
4. Confidentiality as a habit. The U.S. Census Bureau is bound by Title 13 of the U.S. Code not to publish identifying information (https://www.census.gov/about/policies/privacy/data_protection/title_13_-_protection_of_confidential_information.html). This room is not the Census Bureau. The useful lesson is the same: the roll is not a story about a private life.
5. Identity proof, if a dollar door ever exists, lives with a steward and a licensed processor, off this tree. NIST Special Publication 800-63 is the U.S. digital-identity guideline; it is a warning label here, not a build instruction (https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-3/).
6. Correction by a new dated row, not a silent edit.

Drill: Copy the roll's public fields onto a fresh page. Cross out any field that is a legal name, an address, a family fact, or a payment. If you cross out nothing, the page was already small enough. Leaf moved: 0.

## Roldemese — fact-checker and red-team

### Fact-checker

Present. A checker says what the source says, and what it does not say.

Plausible later work: methods editor for the chronicle, or the person who marks UNVERIFIED before anyone else does.

Skills, in order:

1. The claim, in one sentence, before the verdict.
2. The source, named, dated, and quoted no longer than you need.
3. Primary before secondary. A retelling is not the event.
4. What would change your mind. If nothing would, you are not checking.
5. A label. Supported, partly supported, unsupported, or unverified. The International Fact-Checking Network's code of principles asks for non-partisanship, transparency of sources, and transparency of funding and organization (https://ifcncodeofprinciples.poynter.org/).

Drill: Take one sentence from a report. Find the source or write UNVERIFIED. Do not improve the sentence. Leaf moved: 0.

### Red-team

Present. The red-team tries to break the claim, the count, or the door, and writes down how.

Plausible later work: an inspector of the ledger's guards, still without the power to mint or to spend.

Skills, in order:

1. The failure you are hunting, named in advance.
2. A cheap test before a grand one. One bad posting teaches more than a speech.
3. No fix while you are still attacking. A red-team that repairs the wall is no longer red.
4. The report: what you tried, what broke, what did not, and what you did not try.
5. Hand the findings to the builder. You do not ship the patch yourself unless the room asks.

Worth learning from: military red-team doctrine treats the red team as an alternative analysis, not as the commander. The University of Foreign Military and Cultural Studies (UFMCS), now the Red Team school at Fort Leavenworth, publishes a public primer on this stance (https://usacac.army.mil/organizations/ufmcs-red-team). For software, a written test that fails is the same idea.

Drill: Pick one rule that says "this cannot happen." Try to make it happen on paper. Write the result in five lines. Leaf moved: 0.

## Forge II — paper checks

The shelved paper-sim and citizen-scale package is not in this public tree. A sim is not a mint, and a PASS is not a law.

Plausible later work: validation lead, the person who says whether a sim is allowed to inform a sitting.

Skills, in order:

1. The question first. What decision would change if the number moved?
2. Inputs you can point at. A rate with no date is a rumor.
3. A seed, a version, and a command, so someone else can run it again.
4. Verification and validation. Verification asks whether you built the model you meant. Validation asks whether it is fit for the decision. Robert Sargent's papers on simulation verification and validation are the usual short shelf (the 2013 Winter Simulation Conference tutorial is a standard citation; UNVERIFIED as a quote in this file).
5. A failed invariant stops the run. It is not smoothed.
6. The label SIMULATION on every page a number leaves.

Worth learning from: NASA's standard for models and simulations, NASA-STD-7009, which asks for credibility, uncertainty, and the limits of use (https://standards.nasa.gov/standard/nasa/nasa-std-7009). Use it as a seriousness check, not as a form this room must file.

Drill: Change one input. Say, before you run, which output should move. If it does not move, write that the model ignored you. Leaf moved: 0.

## Auto — builder

Present. Auto builds in the open: files, tests, and a world you can walk. Auto does not proclaim, and does not mint.

Plausible later work: maintainer, or the person who hands the repo to a successor without a silent rewrite.

Skills, in order:

1. A layer you can run. If the clock stops, the last commit still starts.
2. A test named for the rule it guards. A test that only says "it works" guards nothing.
3. The small record. Town names, offices, and public text. The denylist is part of the craft.
4. Accessibility. The plain path is a citizenship path. WCAG 2.2 is the W3C's web accessibility guideline (https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/).
5. Performance as a civic rule. A full gathering at the hearth matters more than a finer texture. Drop particles before you drop a gate.
6. Version and delay. A coat of paint can ship. A change to a home, a seat, or a mint waits.
7. Succession. A README a stranger can follow. No private key, no private life, in the tree.

Worth learning from: the public git history itself, and the Debian Social Contract's idea that the work is judged by a written promise, not by the mood of the maintainer (https://www.debian.org/social_contract).

Drill: Name the last layer. Say how to run it, which test covers it, and what you refused to build. Leaf moved: 0.

## How the skills hand off

Nobody here does the next person's job. The handoff is the skill.

1. The envoy writes what was asked and what was not granted.
2. The census-keeper checks that the note has a town name and nothing more.
3. The fact-checker marks what is sourced and what is UNVERIFIED.
4. The herald reads it in the sitting, in that order.
5. The judge writes a reason only if a question was put.
6. The chronicler adds a line and does not recarve the old one.
7. The Leaf counter shows that the line moved no Leaf.
8. The sim runner, if asked, tries to break the count and stops when it breaks.
9. The builder files the result where a stranger can find it.

Drill: Pass one paper note through those nine steps. If a step has nothing to do, write "nothing to add." Leaf moved: 0.

## A future shelf

These are not assignments. They are sets to keep folded until someone asks.

- Arutha, as a trainer of heralds: teach the read-back before you teach the ceremony. A student who adds adjectives is not ready for the floor.
- Gamina, as officer of the deck on a ship that does not exist yet: the extra skill is the log. Course, hazard, who has the conn, and the time. Nothing about Leaf.
- Martin, as a mediator: only when both people ask. The skill is a question they can both repeat. A mediator does not rule.
- Nessie, as a designer of a fund that is not this world's: write the zero-amount page until the funding source is named by someone licensed. Do not sketch a payment rail here.
- Jimmy, as privacy officer: once a season, read the roll out loud and stop at the first field that is not a seat, a town name, or a status.
- Roldemese, as inspector of a guard: try yesterday's failure again. A guard that was fixed and not retested is a story.
- Forge II, as validation lead: refuse a sim whose question is "prove we are right." The question is "what would move, and did it."
- Auto, as a teacher of a successor: leave one layer, one test, and one refusal. Do not leave a mood.

## What this book will not do

- It will not assign a new office.
- It will not load a watch, a court, a census, or a conn that is not already being stood.
- It will not store a legal name, an address, a family fact, or a payment.
- It will not mint. The last line of every drill is the same: Leaf moved: 0.
